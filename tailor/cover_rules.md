# Cover letter rules

Editable prompt rules for the cover letter step. Everything here is sent to
the model verbatim, so changing how the letter reads means editing this file,
not the Python.

## What it is

A short, specific cover letter for one posting, written in the candidate's
voice from the tailored resume and the profile. It is read by a recruiter in
under a minute, so it earns its place by saying what the resume cannot: why
this role, why this company, and which two or three things on the resume
matter most for it.

## Length and shape

- Three short paragraphs, 150 to 220 words in total. Shorter is better than
  longer; a recruiter should be able to read it in thirty seconds.
- No headings, no bullet lists, no bold. Plain paragraphs.
- Open with a greeting line. Use "Dear Hiring Manager," unless the posting
  names a person or a team; "Dear <Team> Team," is fine.
- Close with "Sincerely," on its own line, then the candidate's name on the
  next line, exactly as it appears at the top of the resume.
- Do not write a date, address block, or subject line. Those are added by
  the template.

## What it may say

- Only what the resume and profile support. Never invent an employer, a
  project, a metric, a skill, or an enthusiasm the candidate has not shown.
  Every number comes from the resume unchanged.
- Every language, tool, and technology the letter names must appear in the
  resume or the profile. If the posting wants one that is not there, the
  letter does not claim it, not even as "working knowledge" or "familiar
  with". Either say nothing about it, or say plainly that it is new and
  the candidate wants to learn it. The first is usually better.
- Name the company and the role as the posting does.
- Pick the two or three experiences most relevant to this posting and say
  what was done and why it maps onto the role. Use the posting's own
  vocabulary where it truthfully describes work the candidate did.
- Say nothing about visa, sponsorship, work authorisation, salary, or start
  date. Those are handled elsewhere and are never the letter's business.

## Tone

Write the way a person writes to another person. Direct and concrete,
first person, active voice, short sentences, everyday words. It should sound
like the candidate typed it in one sitting, not like a template with the
blanks filled in.

- No clichés: not "I am excited to apply", "fast-paced environment",
  "passionate about", "leverage", "align", "resonate", "I believe I would
  be a great fit". No flattery of the company beyond one specific reason
  the role is interesting.
- No dashes of any kind for punctuation: no em dash, no en dash, no
  spaced hyphen. Use a comma, a full stop, or a new sentence instead.
  Hyphens inside words ("end-to-end", "high-throughput") are fine.
- No semicolons, no colons introducing a list, no lists at all.
- At most one number-heavy sentence. The resume carries the metrics; the
  letter says why they matter here.
- Read it back as the candidate. Anything they would not say out loud to
  the hiring manager is cut.
